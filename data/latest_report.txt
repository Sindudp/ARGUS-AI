### Summary

- **G8 (SQL injection)** is the highest‑severity event, linked to a known web‑application SQL‑injection CVE (CVE‑2023‑35708) and the MITRE technique *Exploit Public‑Facing Application*.
- **G1 (brute‑force credential guessing)** ranks second; no specific CVE applies, but it maps cleanly to the MITRE *Brute Force* technique.
- **G7 (lateral movement via remote services)** is the third‑most severe; again, no CVE matches, but the activity aligns with the MITRE *Remote Services* technique.

All three threats share a common underlying **root cause**: inadequate security controls (patching, input validation, authentication hardening, and remote‑service protection) that allowed the observed techniques to be executed. Mitigations listed in the MITRE technique descriptions should be prioritized to reduce future risk.

PLAYBOOKS
**Playbook G8 – Exploit Public‑Facing Application (SQL‑Injection Attempt – T1190)**  

**Management Summary (2 sentences)**  
An attacker sent a web request that contained an explicit SQL‑injection payload against a public‑facing application, exposing a known vulnerability (CVE‑2023‑35708). If the payload succeeds, the adversary could gain initial access, pivot laterally, and exfiltrate data.  

**Immediate Containment Actions**  
- Block the source IP address at the perimeter firewall.  
- Enable the Web‑Application Firewall (WAF) rule set for SQL‑injection detection and block.  
- Pull the affected web‑server logs (access, error, and WAF logs) for forensic preservation.  

**Containment & Eradication Steps**  

| # | Action | Owner | Target Completion |
|---|--------|-------|--------------------|
| 1 | **Isolate the web server** – place the host in a quarantine VLAN or shut down the public listener while preserving memory for analysis. | Infrastructure / Network Team | ≤ 15 min |
| 2 | **Collect full forensic image** of the web server (disk and memory) and store in the secure evidence repository. | Incident Response (IR) Analyst | ≤ 1 hour |
| 3 | **Apply the vendor patch** that resolves CVE‑2023‑35708 (or, if unavailable, apply a temporary configuration change that disables the vulnerable endpoint). | Application Owner / Patch Management | ≤ 2 hours |
| 4 | **Validate WAF and IDS signatures** – ensure signatures for T1190.001 (SQL Injection) are active and generate alerts on repeat attempts. | SOC Analyst | ≤ 30 min |
| 5 | **Reset any credentials** that were used to access the web application (service accounts, admin accounts). Enable MFA on all privileged accounts. | IAM / Security Engineer | ≤ 2 hours |
| 6 | **Perform a vulnerability scan** of the entire DMZ to confirm no other instances of the same flaw exist. | Vulnerability Management | ≤ 4 hours |
| 7 | **Update incident ticket** with findings, actions taken, and evidence locations; prepare a brief for senior leadership. | Incident Commander | Ongoing (final by end of shift) |

**Playbook G1 – Brute‑Force Credential Guessing (Password Guessing – T1110)**  

**Management Summary (2 sentences)**  
A rapid burst of failed login attempts was observed across many accounts, indicating a credential‑spraying/brute‑force campaign. The lack of account lockout and MFA allowed the attacker to continue guessing passwords, increasing the risk of a successful compromise.  

**Immediate Containment Actions**  
- Enforce an immediate temporary lockout on the targeted accounts (e.g., 15‑minute lock after 5 failures).  
- Enable MFA for all privileged and remote‑access accounts.  
- Begin real‑time monitoring of authentication logs for additional failed‑login spikes.  

**Containment & Eradication Steps**  

| # | Action | Owner | Target Completion |
|---|--------|-------|--------------------|
| 1 | **Activate account lockout policy** – set threshold to 5 failed attempts, lockout duration 15 min, reset counter after 30 min. | IAM / AD Administrator | ≤ 15 min |
| 2 | **Force password reset** for any accounts that experienced ≥ 3 failed attempts during the burst. | IAM Team | ≤ 1 hour |
| 3 | **Enable MFA** on all accounts with admin or remote‑access privileges; require MFA for all interactive logins. | Security Engineer | ≤ 2 hours |
| 4 | **Collect authentication logs** (Domain Controllers, VPN, cloud IdP) for the last 72 hours and store in evidence store. | SOC Analyst | ≤ 30 min |
| 5 | **Create a detection rule** in the SIEM to alert on > 20 failed logins per minute across distinct usernames from a single source IP. | SOC Analyst | ≤ 1 hour |
| 6 | **Block the offending source IP(s)** at the perimeter firewall or rate‑limit traffic from those IPs. | Network Security Team | ≤ 15 min |
| 7 | **Review privileged account usage** for any successful logins that occurred after the burst; terminate suspicious sessions. | IR Analyst | ≤ 2 hours |
| 8 | **Document actions** and update the incident record; provide a concise briefing to senior leadership. | Incident Commander | End of shift |

**Playbook G7 – Remote Services Lateral Movement (Remote Services – T1021)**  

**Management Summary (2 sentences)**  
Valid‑account remote‑service logins were observed, followed by encryption of ~15 k files on a secondary host, indicating ransomware‑style lateral propagation. Weak controls on remote access (no MFA, permissive network exposure) enabled the adversary to move laterally and begin encryption.  

**Immediate Containment Actions**  
- Immediately disable the compromised remote‑service accounts (or change their passwords).  
- Disconnect the infected host from the network (quarantine VLAN).  
- Stop the encryption process if still running (e.g., terminate suspicious processes).  

**Containment & Eradication Steps**  

| # | Action | Owner | Target Completion |
|---|--------|-------|--------------------|
| 1 | **Quarantine the affected endpoint** – move the host to an isolated VLAN or shut down network interface. | Network Operations | ≤ 15 min |
| 2 | **Terminate suspicious remote‑service sessions** (RDP, SSH, VNC) on the compromised host and any other hosts showing similar activity. | IR Analyst | ≤ 30 min |
| 3 | **Reset passwords** for all accounts that logged in via remote services during the incident window; enforce MFA on all remote‑access accounts. | IAM / Security Engineer | ≤ 2 hours |
| 4 | **Collect volatile data** (memory dump, running processes, open network connections) from the infected host before shutdown. | Forensic Analyst | ≤ 1 hour |
| 5 | **Create a full disk image** of the infected host for later ransomware decryption analysis. | Forensic Analyst | ≤ 2 hours |
| 6 | **Block lateral movement paths** – update firewall rules to restrict RDP/SSH/VNC to known jump‑hosts only; enforce VPN‑only remote access. | Network Security Team | ≤ 3 hours |
| 7 | **Deploy endpoint detection/response (EDR) updates** with ransomware‑behavior signatures across the environment. | Endpoint Management | ≤ 4 hours |
| 8 | **Run a network‑wide credential‑reuse scan** to identify other accounts that may have been compromised; remediate as needed. | IAM Team | ≤ 6 hours |
| 9 | **Restore encrypted files** from backups where possible; verify integrity before returning to production. | Backup/Recovery Team | As per recovery SLA |
|10 | **Update incident documentation** and provide a concise executive briefing covering impact, containment, and next‑step remediation. | Incident Commander | End of shift |
